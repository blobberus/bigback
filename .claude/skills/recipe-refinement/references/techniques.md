# Techniques the home cook can use

Each lever below has three parts:
- **What it fixes**
- **How** (with numbers)
- **Watch out**

Prefer one of these to adding ingredients. When you add a lever to a step, put the
one-line *why* in `tip`. Tags point to `sources.md`.

## Salt and moisture
- **Dry brining.**
  - Fixes: dry or bland meat, poor crust, flabby skin.
  - How: salt (adjustments §2 rates) and rest uncovered on a rack in the fridge. Steaks
    and chops need ≥1 hr, chicken 6–24 hr.
  - Watch out: salt either right before cooking or ≥40 min ahead. At 10–30 min the
    surface is wet with brine [S8][S10]. Fish needs ≤15 min [S12].
- **Wet brining.**
  - Fixes: very lean cuts that dry out (breasts, chops, turkey).
  - How: see the adjustments §2 table.
  - Watch out: it hurts browning and skin crispness, and over-time brining makes meat
    salty. Skip it for "enhanced" meat [S6].
- **Salting watery vegetables.**
  - Fixes: soggy gratins, watery salads, eggplant that won't brown.
  - How: ½–1 tsp per lb, 30 min–4 hr in a colander, then pat dry [C19].
- **Baking-soda tenderizing (velveting).**
  - Fixes: tough stir-fry meat, rubbery shrimp.
  - How: 1 tsp soda per 12 oz sliced meat (¼ tsp per 12 oz ground) for 15 min, then rinse
    if the recipe allows [M10]. Shrimp: 1 tsp salt + ¼ tsp soda per lb.

## Heat and browning
- **Preheat and manage the pan.**
  - Fixes: no crust, sticking, gray meat.
  - How: preheat until the oil shimmers. Lay the food away from you. Don't move it until
    it releases.
  - Watch out: wet food can't get above 212°F, so dry it first. Browning needs the
    surface well above that [M6].
- **Batches, not crowding.**
  - Fixes: steaming, soggy stir-fries, gray ground meat, greasy frying.
  - How: leave ~1 inch between pieces, or work in batches and keep the cooked food warm
    on a rack. Doubled recipes need more batches, not a fuller pan [B25][C21].
- **Fat choice by heat.**
  - High-heat searing: refined oils (canola ~400–475°F, peanut ~450°F, avocado).
  - Butter (~300–350°F) and extra-virgin olive oil (~320–410°F) are for lower heat or
    finishing.
  - Smoke-point charts vary *(unverified ranges)*.
- **Reverse sear.**
  - Fixes: thick steaks, chops or roasts with a gray band, or burnt outside and raw
    inside.
  - How: cook in a 200–275°F oven until 10–15°F below the target, then sear hard 45–60 s
    per side. It needs little rest [M5].
- **Sear, then oven.**
  - Fixes: thick pieces that burn before the center is done.
  - How: brown on the stovetop, then finish in a 325–350°F oven to the pull temp [M13].
- **Cold-pan rendering.**
  - Fixes: flabby or fatty skin (thighs, duck, bacon, pork belly).
  - How: lay skin-side down in a cold pan and bring up to medium so the fat renders
    before the surface sets. Weight domed pieces [M13].
- **Crisp-skin setup for roast poultry.**
  - How: dry-brine uncovered in the fridge ≥12 hr. Add ~1 tsp baking powder (not soda)
    to the salt. Poke holes over the fat deposits. Roast hot (450 → 500°F for a whole
    chicken). Sauce goes under the skin, never over it [M12].
- **Two-stage oven, covered then uncovered.**
  - Fixes: casseroles or braises that dry on top or never brown.
  - How: cover with foil to heat through, then uncover for the last 10–20 min to brown.

## Doneness and resting
- **Thermometer and pull temps.**
  - Fixes: any dry, overcooked, raw or "pink" complaint.
  - How: state the pull temp in the step and the final temp in `tip` (adjustments §5).
    Thicker items and hotter cooking carry over more [M4].
- **Resting is about temperature, not juiciness.**
  - A controlled test found rested meat no juicier once core temperature was matched
    [M8]. Rest only as long as carryover needs: about 3–5 min for steaks and chops,
    10–20 min for roasts.
  - Rest uncovered, or loosely covered, so the crust doesn't steam [M6].
  - Fried chicken: a 20-min rest finishes the cooking and firms the crust [M14].
- **Collagen time.**
  - Fixes: tough braises and stews.
  - How: cook gently (a 180–190°F simmer, or a 300–325°F covered oven) until
    fork-tender, around 195–205°F internal. Judge by feel, not the clock. A hard boil
    toughens the meat and clouds the liquid [M22][C29].

## Building flavor
- **Fond and deglazing.**
  - Fixes: flat pan sauces and stews.
  - How: brown the meat and leave the browned bits. Pour off excess fat. Deglaze with
    ~1 cup stock or wine per 12-inch pan and scrape [C4].
  - Watch out: nonstick pans and crowding make little fond. Burnt fond is bitter, so
    lower the heat.
- **Blooming spices.**
  - Fixes: raw, dusty spice flavor, or weak chile heat.
  - How: stir ground spices in warm fat 30 s–1 min, until fragrant. Spice pastes take a
    few minutes, until they darken. Whole spices go in before ground ones [C24][C25].
  - Watch out: they scorch fast and turn bitter.
- **Toasting.**
  - How: toast nuts, seeds and whole spices in a dry pan over medium, shaking, until
    fragrant and a shade darker. Take them out of the pan right away so they don't
    carry over *(heuristic times)*.
- **Aromatic timing.**
  - Onions: sweat 5–8 min to soft and translucent. True caramelizing takes about 1¼ hr.
    The quick method adds water and covers the pot first [C27].
  - Garlic: goes in when the onions are nearly soft, for 30–60 s, then the liquid. A
    garlic-only base starts in a cold pan [C26].
  - Tomato paste: cook 5–7 min until brick-red and sticking [C28].
- **Layered salt and finishing acid.**
  - How: salt in stages (each component as it goes in). Use cooking acid early and fresh
    acid at the end. Taste at the end and adjust (Nosrat) [S12][S13].
- **Umami stacking.**
  - How: add one booster (adjustments §3) at the aromatic stage. Fish sauce can go in
    twice, early and late [S29].

## Sauces and emulsions
- **Mounting with butter (monter au beurre).**
  - Fixes: thin or harsh pan sauces.
  - How: off the heat, whisk in cold butter 1 tbsp at a time, 2–3 tbsp per ¾ cup of
    reduced liquid. Don't boil afterward; it breaks above about 160°F [C2][C3].
- **Broken-sauce rescue.**
  - How: add ¼ cup water, bring to a vigorous simmer while whisking, then reduce again
    [C1].
  - Broken mayo: whisk it slowly into 4 tsp water [C6].
- **Stable vinaigrette.**
  - How: 3 oil : 1 acid. Add ½ tsp Dijon + ½ tsp mayo per tbsp vinegar, and drizzle in
    the oil slowly [C5].
- **Cheese melting.**
  - How: young melting cheese, or a béchamel or starch base, or sodium citrate. Add
    cheese off the heat in handfuls [C7][C9].
- **Slurries and roux.** See adjustments §9 for amounts.
  - Cornstarch: boil 1 min, then stop.
  - Roux and béchamel: whisk in cold milk, then simmer 2 min [C17].

## Starches
- **Pasta water and finishing in the pan.**
  - Fixes: sauce that won't cling, watery plates, clumping.
  - How: salt the water to 0.5–1%. Pull the pasta 1–2 min early. Reserve 1 cup of
    water. Toss in the sauce 1–2 min with a splash of water plus fat, stirring hard. No
    oil in the water [C12][C13].
  - Small pot or cold start (1–2 qt per lb) makes starchier water, so use less of it
    [C12][C14].
- **Rice method.**
  - How: rinse until nearly clear and use the type's ratio. Cook covered on low, then
    rest 10 min with the lid on [C15][C16].
  - Fried rice needs chilled rice.
- **Roasting vegetables.**
  - How: dry them, toss with 1–2 tbsp oil per lb, and spread in one spaced layer on a
    sturdy sheet at 425–500°F (by vegetable). Don't flip until the underside browns
    [C20].
- **Blanch and shock.**
  - How: boil in heavily salted water 2–3 min, then ice water. Finish to order [C22].
- **Stir-fry order.**
  - How: oil just smoking, protein in batches and removed, then vegetables, aromatics
    last for 30 s, then the sauce [C21].

## Frying
- **Temperature control.**
  - How: state a hold range and fry small batches. Let the oil recover between batches.
  - Ranges: fried chicken 305–325°F, fries 325°F then 350°F, tempura 340–350°F
    [M14][M15][M23].
- **Drain and hold.**
  - How: drain on a rack immediately, uncovered. Most oil uptake happens as food leaves
    the oil [M20].
- **Coatings.**
  - How: pat dry. Bread in flour → egg → crumbs, then rest 10–15 min. Replace some flour
    with cornstarch. Vodka or seltzer in the batter limits gluten for a lighter, crisper
    crust [M14][M16].

## Baking
- **Weighing.**
  - Fixes: dense, dry or tough baking, and inconsistent results.
  - How: give grams (KA: 120 g AP flour per cup) plus "spoon and level". Scooped flour
    runs up to 160 g per cup [B1][B2].
- **Ingredient temperature.**
  - How: room temperature is 68–75°F. Cold eggs warm up in 10 min in warm water. Cold
    dairy curdles creamed batters [B3][B6].
- **Mixing methods.**
  - Creaming: 2–3 min. More breaks the emulsion and cookies spread.
  - Muffin method: stir until just combined; lumps are fine.
  - Overmixing after the flour goes in makes it tough [B6][B7][B9].
- **Chilling dough.**
  - How: ≥30 min cuts cookie spread, and most of the gain comes in the first 30 min.
    Keep pie dough cold [B8].
- **Preheating and oven accuracy.**
  - How: preheat 20 min (≤350°F) to 30 min (hotter); the beep is early. Use the middle
    rack and rotate the pan. An oven thermometer finds a 25–50°F error [B18][B23].
- **Doneness by temperature.**
  - How: give the internal temp for bread, cakes and custards (adjustments §7). Cool
    before slicing [B21][B22][B31].
