# Diagnostics: symptom → cause → fix

Each entry has three parts:
- **Check** lists the likely causes, most common first. Look for each one in the
  recipe's own text.
- **Fix** gives recipe changes. Try the lightest lever first.
- **Rescue** is what the cook can do to an already-cooked batch. Put it in the
  resolution, or in a `tip` if it'll recur.

Magnitudes are in `adjustments.md` and levers in `techniques.md`. Tags are listed in
`sources.md`.

Always check first:
- Salt brand vs recipe, and whether the dish was scaled.
- Whether the pan or pot size matches.
- Heat level words ("medium-high").
- Personal-log notes from the same cook ("used Morton", "doubled it", "new oven").

## Seasoning

**Too salty**
- Check:
  1. Morton or table salt used on a Diamond Crystal recipe (~1.7–2× the salt by volume).
  2. Stacked salty components: soy, fish sauce, parmesan, bacon, regular stock, canned
     beans.
  3. Reduction concentrated the salt (regular stock, a long simmer, a reduced pan sauce).
  4. Salted early in something that later reduced.
  5. A wet brine ran over time, then salt was added on top.
  6. Scaled up, which multiplied the salt while evaporation stayed the same.
- Fix:
  - Brand mismatch: fix the note, not the qty.
  - Otherwise cut the main source 20–33%.
  - Switch to low-sodium stock, or count hidden salt (1 tbsp soy ≈ 0.4 tsp table salt).
  - Move the salting after the reduction and add "taste, then salt" [S15][C4].
- Rescue: dilute with more unsalted base, or balance with acid, fat or a little
  sweetness. A potato doesn't pull salt out [S17].

**Bland, tastes like nothing**
- Check:
  1. Salt is below ~1% of the food's weight.
  2. All the salt went in at the start of a long cook.
  3. Unsalted components were never seasoned (pasta water, beans, potatoes).
  4. Low-sodium stock with nothing to make up for it.
- Fix: +25% salt, or season each component in its own step ("salt the potatoes as they
  go in"). Add a final "taste and adjust" cue [S12][S14].

**Flat or heavy, salty enough but dull**
- Check:
  1. No acid in the dish.
  2. Acid only cooked in early.
  3. Rich dairy or fat with nothing to cut it.
- Fix: add finishing acid at the end: lemon, vinegar, or a splash of the dish's own
  wine. Start at ½ tsp per cup of sauce. Herbs at the end [S13][C30].
- Tell flat from bland: taste a spoonful with a pinch of salt and another with a few drops
  of lemon. The one that lifts it is the fix.

**Too sweet**
- Check:
  1. Too much sugar, honey or ketchup.
  2. Sweet vegetables that reduced (onions, carrots).
  3. Sweetened stock or sauce products.
- Fix: cut the sugar 25–33%, or add acid and a little heat or bitterness. Avoid more salt:
  it suppresses bitterness, so the sweetness stands out more [S15][S16][S19].

**Too sour or sharp**
- Check:
  1. Too much citrus or vinegar.
  2. Acid added off the heat at the start, when it should have been tasted in at the end.
  3. Tomatoes that weren't cooked down.
  4. Wine that wasn't reduced before the dairy went in.
- Fix: cut the acid 25%, or hold some back "to taste". A pinch of sugar (¼ tsp per 3 cups)
  or 1 tbsp butter rounds it. Baking soda dulls it, and too much tastes soapy [S18].
- If the note reverses an earlier "more acid" change, follow the ping-pong rule in SKILL.md.

**Bitter or acrid**
- Check:
  1. Garlic or spices scorched. Minced garlic burns in under a minute on high heat.
  2. Burnt fond.
  3. Over-toasted nuts or spices.
  4. Pith or zest overdone.
  5. Over-steeped tea or herbs.
  6. Charred bits.
- Fix: put garlic in later and over lower heat. Bloom spices 30 s in warm fat. Lower the
  fond heat. Salt and a little acid tame residual bitterness [C24][C25][C26][S19].
- Rescue: badly burnt garlic means start over.

**Too spicy**
- Check:
  1. Fresh chile heat varies, and ribs or seeds were left in.
  2. Chile bloomed in oil, which extracts more than 2× the capsaicin.
  3. Chile added late (cooking tames it).
  4. Hot chile powder used in place of mild.
- Fix:
  - Cut the chile 33–50%, or seed it.
  - Cook it longer (5 min cuts the burn).
  - Make it adjustable: "add half, taste, then the rest" [S20][S21].
- Rescue: dairy, sugar or lime, or dilute. Water doesn't help [S22].

**Not spicy enough**
- Check:
  1. Mild or old chiles.
  2. Chile simmered a long time.
  3. Only a pinch.
- Fix: increase the dried chile ~50%, or bloom it in the fat early. Add fresh chile or
  flakes at the end for brightness [S20][S21].

**Lacks depth or savoriness**
- Check:
  1. No browning (the meat steamed, the pan was crowded).
  2. Fond discarded.
  3. Tomato paste not browned.
  4. No umami source.
- Fix: brown harder (see "No crust" below), deglaze, and cook tomato paste 5–7 min until
  it darkens. Add one umami booster: anchovy, fish sauce, soy, parmesan or MSG [S27][C28].

## Meat and protein texture

**Dry (lean meat: breast, pork chop, tenderloin, fish)**
- Check:
  1. Overcooked, because the recipe gives only a time and no temperature.
  2. Pulled at the final temperature without allowing for carryover (+5–15°F).
  3. Uneven thickness.
  4. Very hot oven, which brings bigger carryover.
  5. Lean cut, unbrined.
- Fix:
  - Add pull temperatures plus a thermometer cue (adjustments §5).
  - Pound or butterfly to an even thickness.
  - Add a dry brine (≥40 min) or a wet brine.
  - Lower the oven, or switch to a reverse sear [M3][M4][M8][S10].

**Tough or stringy (braises, stews, shoulder, short ribs)**
- Check:
  1. Undercooked collagen: pulled by the clock while still tough (it needs ~195–205°F
     and time).
  2. A hard boil, which toughens the outside of the meat.
  3. A lean cut braised.
- Fix:
  - Extend to "fork-tender", with a probe cue.
  - Simmer below a boil, or use a covered 300–325°F oven, or a McGee-style 200–250°F
    oven with the lid ajar.
  - Name a collagen-rich cut [M22][C29].

**Tough (steaks, quick-cooked cuts)**
- Check:
  1. Sliced with the grain (more than 4× the bite force).
  2. Overcooked.
  3. A chewy cut cooked past medium.
- Fix: add "slice thinly against the grain" and a pull temp. For stir-fry, add a baking
  soda tenderizer (1 tsp per 12 oz sliced meat, 15 min) [M19][M10].

**Rubbery (shrimp, squid, chicken breast)**
- Check:
  1. Overcooked by a minute or two.
  2. Crowded and stewed.
- Fix: a shorter time with a visual cue ("just opaque"). Cook shrimp in batches over high
  heat. Brine shrimp (1 tsp salt + ¼ tsp baking soda per lb, 15 min).

**Dense or springy burgers or meatballs**
- Check:
  1. Salt mixed into the ground meat early.
  2. Overworked.
  3. Too lean.
- Fix: salt only the outside right before cooking. Mix gently. Use 80/20 beef. A panade
  for meatballs.

## Browning, crust, skin

**No crust, gray, pale browning**
- Check:
  1. Wet surface (a wet surface can't get above 212°F).
  2. Pan not preheated.
  3. Crowding (the temperature drops and the meat steams).
  4. Flipped or moved too soon.
  5. Wet brine.
  6. Nonstick pan, or heat too low.
- Fix:
  - Pat dry, or salt ≥40 min ahead uncovered.
  - Preheat until the oil shimmers.
  - Leave space between pieces and cook in batches.
  - "Don't move until it releases" [M6][S10][C21].

**Skin not crisp, flabby**
- Check:
  1. Moisture: not dried, or wet-brined.
  2. Fat not rendered: heat too high (surface burns first) or too short.
  3. Domed pieces losing pan contact (flabby center).
  4. Covered, tented, basted or sauced over the skin.
  5. Rested under foil.
- Fix:
  - Dry brine uncovered in the fridge 6–24 hr; baking powder in the salt for roasting.
  - Cold-pan start, skin-down, over medium.
  - Weight the pieces.
  - Raise the oven for the finish (450→500°F for a whole bird).
  - Sauce under, not over. Rest uncovered [M11][M12][M13][M6].

**Burnt outside, raw inside**
- Check:
  1. Heat too high for the thickness.
  2. Too thick or uneven.
  3. Fridge-cold center in a thick cut (minor).
  4. Sugar in the marinade or glaze burning.
- Fix:
  - Lower the heat.
  - Sear, then finish in a 325–350°F oven, or reverse sear.
  - Cut, pound or butterfly thinner.
  - Glaze only in the last minutes.
  - Remember time scales with thickness² [M13][M21].

**Overcooked outside, gray band, center just right**
- Check: very hot oven or grill the whole time.
- Fix: reverse sear (low oven to 10–15°F under target, then a hard sear), or lower the
  oven [M4][M5].

## Moisture and vegetables

**Soggy or watery (gratins, stir-fries, roasted veg, casseroles)**
- Check:
  1. Watery vegetables not salted and drained (zucchini, eggplant, tomato, mushrooms,
     cabbage).
  2. Crowded sheet pan or wok.
  3. Lid on when it should be off.
  4. Frozen vegetables not thawed and dried.
  5. Wet vegetables went in.
- Fix:
  - Salt and drain (½–1 tsp/lb, 30 min, pat dry).
  - One spaced layer at 425–500°F.
  - Stir-fry in batches over high heat.
  - Reduce uncovered.
  - Mash some of the starch already in the pot [C19][C20][C21][C23].

**Mushy vegetables**
- Check:
  1. Overcooked.
  2. Cut too small for the cook time.
  3. Added at the same time as slower items.
  4. Held hot.
- Fix:
  - Shorter time with a texture cue.
  - Bigger cuts.
  - Stagger additions (dense first, tender last).
  - Blanch 2–3 min and shock, then finish to order [C22].

**Vegetables raw or crunchy in the middle**
- Fix:
  - Cut smaller or more evenly.
  - Cover with foil for the first 10–15 min of roasting dense vegetables.
  - Par-cook [C20].

**Watery soup, stew or chili**
- Check:
  1. Too much liquid for the pot's evaporation.
  2. Covered the whole time.
  3. Slow cooker (it can't evaporate).
  4. Doubled liquid in a scaled batch.
- Fix: cut the liquid 15–25%, simmer uncovered for the last 20–30 min, mash some beans or
  potatoes, or thicken (adjustments §9) [C23][B25].

## Sauces

**Sauce thin or watery**
- Check:
  1. Not reduced enough (the target volume isn't stated).
  2. Juices from resting meat poured in.
  3. Cornstarch boiled too long (it thins again).
  4. Butter sauce got too hot.
- Fix:
  - Give a target ("reduced to ¾ cup" or "coats a spoon").
  - Add a small starch: 1 tsp cornstarch slurry per cup, or a beurre manié.
  - Mount butter off the heat [C2][C4][C17].

**Sauce too thick or gluey**
- Check:
  1. Too much starch or roux.
  2. Over-reduced.
  3. It sat and set (starch sauces thicken as they cool).
- Fix: cut the thickener 25–33%. Finish with "thin with stock or pasta water a spoonful
  at a time" [C13].

**Broken, greasy or oily sauce (pan sauces, butter sauces, emulsions)**
- Check:
  1. Too much water evaporated.
  2. Butter added warm or all at once.
  3. Sauce boiled after the butter (it breaks above ~160°F).
  4. Too much fat for the liquid.
- Fix:
  - Add cold butter off the heat, 1 tbsp at a time.
  - State the reduced volume before the butter goes in.
  - Add "if it looks greasy, whisk in a splash of water" [C1][C2][C3].
- Rescue: ¼ cup water, a vigorous simmer while whisking, then reduce again. Flour won't
  fix it [C1].
- Broken mayo or vinaigrette: whisk the broken sauce slowly into 4 tsp water [C6], or
  add mustard or mayo as a stabilizer [C5].

**Grainy or clumpy cheese sauce**
- Check:
  1. Aged cheese only (sharp cheddar, Gruyère).
  2. Too hot or boiled.
  3. Acid present.
  4. Low-fat cheese.
  5. Added all at once.
- Fix:
  - Blend in a young melter (Jack, American) or add a starch base (béchamel).
  - Add the cheese off the heat in handfuls.
  - Or use sodium citrate (2.8% of the cheese + liquid weight) [C7][C8][C9].

**Curdled cream or yogurt sauce**
- Check:
  1. Low-fat dairy boiled.
  2. Acid not reduced first.
  3. Yogurt or sour cream simmered (it curdles near 185°F).
- Fix: use full fat (heavy cream survives a boil). Reduce the wine or acid fully first.
  Stir cultured dairy in off the heat [C10].

## Pasta and rice

**Gummy or sticky pasta**
- Check:
  1. Overcooked.
  2. Sat in the colander.
  3. Too little water without stirring.
  4. Rinsed and then sauced hot.
- Fix: pull 1–2 min early and finish in the sauce. Stir in the first minute. Have the
  sauce ready before the pasta is [C13].

**Sauce won't cling, or it's watery on the plate**
- Check:
  1. No pasta water or fat.
  2. Not finished in the pan.
  3. Oil in the cooking water.
- Fix: toss in the sauce for 1–2 min with a splash of starchy water plus butter or oil,
  stirring hard. Drop the oil from the water [C13].

**Pasta salty or bland**
- Fix: water at 0.5–1% salt (adjustments §2). With salty sauces like puttanesca or
  carbonara, go toward 0.5%.

**Cacio e pepe or cheese clumps**
- Fix: use less, starchier water. Make a cheese paste with warm water and toss off the
  heat, keeping it between 145 and 180°F [C11].

**Mushy or gluey rice**
- Check:
  1. Too much water, often from scaling linearly.
  2. Not rinsed.
  3. Lid lifted, or no rest.
  4. Stirred.
- Fix:
  - Water at the type's ratio (adjustments §9).
  - Rinse.
  - "Rest covered 10 min, don't lift the lid."
  - For scaled batches, increase the water less than the rice [C15][C16].

**Crunchy or undercooked rice**
- Check:
  1. Too little water.
  2. Lid not tight.
  3. Heat too high, so the water evaporated.
- Fix: the correct ratio, the lowest heat, a tight lid, and a 10-min rest.
- Rescue: a splash of hot water, covered on low.

**Soggy fried rice**
- Fix: use chilled day-old rice, or spread fresh rice on a sheet, cool 10 min, then chill
  20 min. Hot wok, small batches.

## Frying

**Greasy fried food**
- Check:
  1. Oil temperature not stated or not held.
  2. Pot overloaded (the temperature crashes).
  3. Drained on paper or in a pile.
  4. An all-flour coating.
- Fix:
  - State the oil temp and the hold range (fried chicken 305–325°F, fries 325 then 350°F,
    tempura 340–350°F).
  - Fry in small batches with recovery time.
  - Drain on a rack immediately.
  - Use cornstarch for part of the flour [M14][M15][M20].
- Caveat: oil uptake mostly happens as food comes out and cools. Prompt draining matters
  more than hotter oil [M20].

**Soggy coating, or coating falls off**
- Check:
  1. Surface wet.
  2. Coating not rested.
  3. Oil too cool.
  4. Crowded.
  5. Covered after frying.
- Fix: pat dry, go flour → egg → crumb, and rest breaded pieces 10–15 min on a rack. A
  vodka or seltzer batter with cornstarch stays crisper. Hold on a rack, uncovered
  [M15][M16].

**Browned outside, raw inside (fried)**
- Fix: lower the oil to the recipe's hold range, cut smaller, or finish in a 350°F oven.
  Give pull temps (white meat 145°F, dark 160°F, then rest) [M14].

## Baking

**Dense or heavy cake or quick bread**
- Check:
  1. Too much flour (scooped: +20–33%).
  2. Overmixed after the flour went in.
  3. Under-creamed.
  4. Cold dairy or eggs.
  5. Old leavener.
- Fix: grams plus "spoon and level". "Mix just until no dry flour remains." Room-temp
  ingredients (68–75°F). A leavener freshness cue [B2][B4][B6][B12].

**Cookies spread flat**
- Check:
  1. Butter too warm or melted.
  2. Too little flour.
  3. Over-creamed.
  4. Hot or greased pan.
  5. Oven off.
  6. Too much sugar.
- Fix:
  - Chill the dough ≥30 min.
  - Weigh the flour.
  - Cream only 2–3 min.
  - Use a cool pan with parchment.
  - Cut sugar 1–2 tbsp if it keeps happening [B7][B8].

**Cookies cakey or puffy (wanted chewy)**
- Fix:
  - Melted butter.
  - An extra yolk instead of a whole egg.
  - More brown sugar.
  - Soda-led leavening.
  - Pull slightly underbaked [B29].

**Dry cake or muffins**
- Check:
  1. Overbaked (no temp or test cue).
  2. Too much flour.
  3. Pan larger than written.
  4. Oven hot.
- Fix: check 5 min early, with a done temp (cakes 200–209°F, quick breads 200–205°F).
  Weigh the flour. Name the pan size.
- Rescue: brush layers with simple syrup [B4][B31].

**Gummy or wet crumb (bread, banana bread, cake center)**
- Check:
  1. Underbaked.
  2. Sliced hot.
  3. Too much wet add-in (fruit).
  4. Underproofed bread.
- Fix: add a done temp (lean bread 200–210°F, enriched 185–195°F, quick breads
  200–205°F) and "cool at least 1 hr (full loaves ~3 hr) before slicing" [B20][B21][B22].

**Sunken center**
- Check:
  1. Underbaked.
  2. Too much leavener or sugar.
  3. Door opened early.
  4. Pan too small or too full (over ⅔).
  5. Oven too cool.
- Fix:
  - Bake to temp and spring-back.
  - Check the leavening against 1–1¼ tsp baking powder per cup of flour.
  - Name the pan.
  - "Don't open the oven before ¾ of the bake time" [B5][B34].

**Tough or rubbery muffins, scones, biscuits, pie crust**
- Check:
  1. Overmixed or overworked (gluten).
  2. Too much water in the pastry.
  3. Left in the hot pan.
- Fix: "Stir until just combined; lumps are fine." Keep the fat cold for pastry. Use the
  pie dough ratio of 3 flour : 2 fat : 1 water. Unmold muffins promptly [B9].

**Pale or under-browned**
- Check:
  1. Oven cool or not fully preheated.
  2. Rack too high or low.
  3. Glass or light pan.
  4. Low sugar.
- Fix: preheat 20–30 min, middle rack, rotate the pan. Raise the temp 25°F only if the
  interior is also underdone [B18][B23].

**Over-browned outside, underdone inside**
- Check:
  1. Oven too hot.
  2. Dark or glass pan.
  3. Honey or extra sugar.
- Fix: −25°F for dark or glass pans. Tent with foil once it's browned. Bake to an
  internal temp [B16][B17].

## Timing (the note says a step took longer or shorter)

**"Took way longer than it says"**
- Check:
  1. Heat level wording (medium vs medium-high).
  2. Pan or pot size (a smaller pot means deeper food and slower cooking).
  3. Thicker cut than written.
  4. Scaled batch.
  5. The oven running cool.
- Fix:
  - Re-range the time to what happened (low end = their time if credible).
  - Add the sensory cue so the clock matters less.
  - Update timers and `time.*`.
  - If the heat was the issue, change the heat instead of the time.

**"Done way early"**
- Same checks, reversed. Shorten the range and lead with the cue.

**Undercooked center, everything else fine**
- Fix: add or raise the internal temp target and a carryover-aware pull. For thick items,
  lower the heat and cook longer, or use a sear-then-oven finish (time ∝ thickness²) [M21].
