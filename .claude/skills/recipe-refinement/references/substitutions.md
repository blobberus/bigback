# Substitutions for a basic pantry

These swaps keep a recipe cookable with what a normal kitchen has. How to apply them:
- **Note format.** Put the swap in the ingredient `note` ("or ¾ cup milk + ¼ cup melted
  butter"). Leave the main ingredient as it is, unless the cook asked to replace it or
  the original is genuinely hard to find.
- **Caveats.** If a swap changes texture or flavor, say so in `tip`.

Tags point to `sources.md`. *(heuristic)* means common practice that isn't sourced.

## Dairy and fat
- **Buttermilk, 1 cup:**
  - Milk to 1 cup + 1 tbsp lemon juice or vinegar, rested 10–15 min.
  - Or 1¾ tsp cream of tartar per cup of milk.
  - Best flavor: ⅓ cup Greek yogurt (75 g) + ⅔ cup milk.
  - Never plain milk in a soda-leavened recipe; without the acid it turns out dense and
    dark [B10].
- **Heavy cream** (for cooking; it won't whip): ¾ cup milk + ¼ cup melted butter. It's
  less stable when boiled with acid. Real cream survives a boil because it's ≥36% fat
  [B32][C10].
- **Half-and-half:** ¾ cup whole milk + ¼ cup heavy cream [B32].
- **Sour cream ↔ plain Greek yogurt:** 1:1, full-fat; nonfat turns out thin and chalky.
  Both curdle near 185°F, so stir them in off the heat. Crème fraîche can simmer
  [B3][B32][C10].
- **Butter ↔ oil:**
  - 1 cup butter ≈ ¾ cup oil. Butter is ~80% fat and ~16–18% water [B38][C2].
  - Oil → butter: use about 25% more butter [B4].
  - Don't swap in cookies, creamed cakes or pastry, where solid fat gives the structure.
    In cakes, replacing 25–50% of the butter with oil makes them moister (KA, snippet).
- **Salted butter for unsalted:** cut the added salt by ¼ tsp per stick [B3].
- **Pecorino ↔ parmesan:** 1:1. Pecorino is saltier, so ease off the salt *(heuristic)*.

## Flour, sugar, leaveners, eggs
- **Cake flour, 1 cup:**
  - 105 g AP flour + 14 g cornstarch, i.e. 1 cup minus 2 tbsp AP + 2 tbsp cornstarch.
  - The crumb comes out slightly coarser [B13][B26].
- **Self-rising flour, 1 cup:**
  - 120 g AP flour + 1½ tsp baking powder + ¼ tsp table salt.
  - Using store-bought self-rising in a recipe: only when the recipe calls for ½–1 tsp
    baking powder per cup of flour. Leave out the recipe's baking powder and salt [B11].
- **Bread flour ↔ AP flour:** 1:1. AP gives less chew *(heuristic)*.
- **Brown sugar, 1 cup:** 1 cup granulated + 1 tbsp molasses for light, 2 tbsp for dark
  [B28].
- **Powdered sugar, 1 cup:** 1 cup granulated + 1 tsp cornstarch, blended. Fine for
  dusting, poor for icing [B28].
- **Corn syrup, 1 cup:** 1 cup sugar + ¼ cup hot water [B32].
- **Honey or maple for sugar:**
  - Replace at most half the sugar.
  - Cut the recipe's liquid by ¼ cup per cup of honey or maple.
  - Bake 25°F lower [B28][B44].
- **Baking powder, 1 tsp:** ¼ tsp baking soda + ½ tsp cream of tartar. It reacts at once,
  so bake immediately [B32][B36].
- **Baking soda, 1 tsp:** about 3 tsp baking powder. Expect less browning and a different
  flavor. It won't neutralize much acid [B32][B36].
- **1 egg in baking:**
  - 1 tbsp ground flax + 3 tbsp water, rested 5 min.
  - Or 3 tbsp aquafaba.
  - Or ¼ cup applesauce, mashed banana or yogurt.
  - The result is denser. Don't use these for custards, soufflés or omelets [B32][B38].
- **Half an egg:** whisk the egg, then weigh about 25 g (a large egg is ~50 g) [B14].
- **Chocolate** [B27]:
  - 1 oz unsweetened = 3 tbsp cocoa + 1 tbsp oil.
  - 1 oz semisweet = ⅔ oz unsweetened + 2 tsp sugar.
  - Don't replace all the chocolate in brownies.

## Aromatics, herbs, spices, citrus
- **Fresh ↔ dried herbs:**
  - 1 tbsp fresh = 1 tsp dried.
  - Only oregano, thyme, rosemary, sage, marjoram and bay dry well.
  - Basil, parsley, cilantro, dill, chives and mint should stay fresh.
  - Dried herbs go in early with the liquid [S23][S24].
- **Garlic:** 1 clove = ⅛ tsp garlic powder or about ¼ tsp granulated. Powder scorches,
  so add it with the liquid [S25].
- **Shallot ↔ onion:**
  - 1 small shallot ≈ 3 tbsp minced. Swap in an equal volume of minced yellow onion.
  - For raw use, soak the onion in cold water for 10 min to tame it *(heuristic)*.
- **Onion powder:** sources conflict. Start with about 1 tsp per medium onion
  *(unverified)*.
- **Whole ↔ ground spices:** about 1:1. Ground cumin and mustard run up to 1.5× the volume
  of whole (adjustments §4) [S26].
- **Fresh ginger, 1 tbsp:** ¼ tsp ground. It tastes warmer and less bright, so add it
  with the other spices *(heuristic)*.
- **Citrus:**
  - 1 lemon ≈ 3 tbsp juice + 1 tbsp zest. 1 lime ≈ 2 tbsp juice *(heuristic)*.
  - Lemon ↔ lime: 1:1.
  - Citrus juice → vinegar: start at ¾ the amount and taste. Vinegar is sharper and has
    no zest aroma.
  - Bottled juice is fine cooked, flat raw.

## Liquids for sauces, braises, soups
- **Wine, 1 cup** *(heuristic)*:
  - Low-sodium stock + 1–2 tsp vinegar: white-wine or cider vinegar for white wine, red
    wine vinegar for red.
  - Dry vermouth keeps for months and is the best pantry white wine.
  - Reduce wine fully before adding dairy [C10].
- **Stock** *(heuristic)*:
  - Water + 1 tsp soy sauce or ½ tsp fish sauce per cup, and brown the aromatics harder.
  - Bouillon is salty, so cut the recipe's salt.
  - Prefer low-sodium, because reduction concentrates salt [C4].
- **Vinegars** *(heuristic)*:
  - White wine ↔ cider ↔ sherry vinegar: 1:1.
  - Rice vinegar is milder: use about 1.25×.
  - Distilled white vinegar is harsher: use ¾.
  - Balsamic is sweet. It isn't a straight acid swap.

## Umami and depth (add one, small)
- **Fish sauce ↔ soy sauce:** about 1:1 for salt. Fish sauce is funkier and saltier per
  tbsp, ≈ 0.5–0.65 tsp table salt vs ≈ 0.4 for soy (calc) [S29].
- **Anchovy:** 2 fillets per 3–4 lb of stew, cooked into the aromatics [S27].
- **Others:** a parmesan rind in soup; tomato paste browned 5–7 min [C28]; Worcestershire.
- **MSG:** ¼–½ tsp per 4 servings. Cut the salt by 25–33% if you add it [S28].

## Thickeners
**1 tbsp cornstarch =** 2 tbsp flour = 1½ tbsp arrowroot = 1–1½ tbsp potato starch =
1 tbsp tapioca starch [C17].
- Arrowroot turns slimy with dairy.
- Cornstarch weeps after freezing, so use tapioca or arrowroot for dishes you'll freeze.
- Flour needs a couple of minutes of simmering to lose its raw taste [C17].

## Salt
See the brand conversion table in adjustments §2. DC → Morton ×0.6, DC → table ×0.5,
Morton → table ×0.8 [S2][S3].

## Equipment (offer in `tip`)
All of these are common practice *(heuristic)*:
- **No Dutch oven:** a heavy lidded pot. Put foil under the lid if it's loose.
- **No cast iron:** heavy stainless steel. Preheat it longer and use a little more oil.
- **No wire rack** (for drying or draining): set a cooling rack in a sheet pan, or use a
  bed of crumpled foil. Paper towels steam fried food.
- **No stand mixer:** a hand mixer. Cream 1–2 min longer.
- **No instant-read thermometer:** give the visual cue too ("juices run clear" is
  unreliable for poultry). Suggest the thermometer for any doneness complaint.
- **No food processor:** knife-chop, grate, or crush in a zip bag with a rolling pin.
