# Sources

This file records the research behind the reference files. Pages were fetched and read
in November 2026.

**How to read the tags.** Tags in the other files use prefixes:
- `[S#]` seasoning and salt
- `[M#]` meat, temperatures, browning and frying
- `[C#]` sauces, starches, vegetables and aromatics
- `[B#]` baking, scaling and substitutions

**Access gaps.** Serious Eats, Bon Appétit, NYT Cooking, Epicurious and The Kitchn blocked
automated fetching. Their findings come through mirrors, syndications or secondary write-ups,
and each such line says so.

**Confidence.** In the other files, *(unverified)* or *(heuristic)* marks numbers taken only
from search snippets or low-authority pages, or our own judgment. Treat those as starting
points. Some ATK links are shortened to their article id. If one 404s, search the title.

**Not used.** Sally's Baking Addiction (403) and Food52 (rate-limited) could not be read.

## Seasoning and salt (S)
1. **ATK, "The Wrong Brand of Kosher Salt Could Ruin Your Dinner".** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/5493. Volume ratios between brands; DC ≈ 3 g vs Morton ≈ 5 g per tsp.
2. **Milk Street, "Morton's vs Diamond Crystal".** https://www.177milkstreet.com/stories/10-2020-kosher-salt-brand. Weighed 2.8 g vs 4.8 g per tsp.
3. **Morton Salt, Salt Conversion Chart.** https://www.mortonsalt.com/salt-conversion-chart/. Official 1 tsp table = 1¼ tsp Morton kosher; fine sea salt ≈ table salt.
4. **Chris Morocco (Bon Appétit), "How to Cook a Bon Appétit Recipe".** https://fromthetestkitchen.substack.com/p/how-to-cook-a-bon-appetit-recipe. BA tests with DC; Morton at about half; equal weights of salt give equal salinity.
5. **TASTE, "The Kosher Salt Question".** https://tastecooking.com/kosher-salt-question/. Which publications assume which brand; Nosrat's view.
6. **ATK, salting vs brining chicken.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/610. Dry and wet chicken amounts; brines hurt browning; don't brine enhanced meat.
7. **ATK, "Brining Meat".** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/5804-brining-meat. Full brine chart: strengths, times per cut.
8. **ATK, "How to Salt a Roast and Other Meats".** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/162-how-to-salt-a-roast-and-other-meats. Dry-salt chart with rest times.
9. **ATK, properly seasoned steak.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/5973. About 5 g/lb for steak and 2 g/lb for chicken.
10. **Serious Eats / Kenji López-Alt, "The Food Lab: How to Grill a Steak" (mirror).** https://mywebvault.wordpress.com/2012/05/14/the-food-lab-how-to-grill-a-steak-a-complete/. Salt immediately or ≥40 min ahead; why 10–30 min gives a wet crust.
11. **Mashed, summary of the Serious Eats pasta-water test.** https://www.mashed.com/165870/. Gives 1% salinity per liter by brand; usable range 0.5–2%; "like the sea" is about 3.5%.
12. **Notes on Salt Fat Acid Heat (Samin Nosrat).** https://joekotlan.com/salt-fat-acid-heat/. Salt early and in stages; cooking vs finishing acid; fish ≤15 min; beans salt early, acid late.
13. **The Splendid Table, interview with Samin Nosrat.** https://www.splendidtable.org/story/2017/05/05/. How to tell a dish needs acid ("until it pops").
14. **Nosrat quote, 1% / 2% salinity.** https://quotefancy.com/quote/2907861. Search snippets only: about 1% for meat, vegetables and grains; 2% for blanching or pasta water.
15. **ATK, fixing food that's too salty, sweet, sour or spicy.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/5816. Fixes for each; salt suppresses bitterness.
16. **TODAY, "How to fix food that's too salty, sour, spicy or sweet".** https://www.today.com/food/how-fix-food-s-too-salty-sour-spicy-or-sweet-t105034. Dilution; about 1 tsp vinegar for a flat stew; no salt on too-sweet.
17. **Sara Moulton / Robert Wolke, potato test.** https://saramoulton.com/2012/07/. The potato "absorbs salt" myth is debunked.
18. **ATK, baking soda vs sugar in tomato sauce.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/6605. Sugar ¼ tsp per 3 cups beats soda; add fat 1 tbsp at a time.
19. **Breslin & Beauchamp, "Salt enhances flavour by suppressing bitterness".** *Nature* 1997, https://www.nature.com/articles/42388. The mechanism.
20. **ATK, "Blooming in Oil".** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how-tos/6581. Oil extracts more than 2× the capsaicin and 10× the thymol that water does.
21. **ATK, turning down jalapeño heat.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/8557. 5 minutes of cooking cuts the burn; add fresh chile late.
22. **Phys.org, why water doesn't help with spicy food.** https://phys.org/news/2024-06-doesnt-spicy-food-beer.html. Capsaicin solubility; milk research.
23. **ATK, substituting dried herbs.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how-tos/9261. 1:3 dried:fresh, tested by herb.
24. **ATK, when to use dried herbs.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/5397. Which herbs dry well; add at the same time as fresh.
25. **McCormick, "Flavor Story: Garlic Powder".** https://www.mccormick.com/blogs/how-to/flavor-story-garlic-powder. ⅛ tsp = 1 clove.
26. **Milk Street, "Taking the Measure".** https://www.177milkstreet.com/stories/taking-the-measure. Measured whole → ground spice volumes.
27. **Cook's Country, "Umami Powerhouses".** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/cookscountry/how_tos/8173. Anchovy, tomato paste, Marmite and kombu doses.
28. **Ajinomoto, "When sodium is not sodium".** https://www.ajihealthandnutrition.com/media/when-sodium-is-not-sodium/. MSG is 12% sodium vs 39% for salt; dosing. This is a vendor source.
29. **Special Sauce podcast, Kenji on fish sauce.** https://www.thespecialsaucepodcast.com/. Add a splash early and a splash late.

Also used, snippets only and unverified:
- Sensory difference thresholds for salt: about 12% in mashed potato, 14% in mayonnaise, 28% in tomato sauce.
- Kenji: salt burger patties only on the outside; tomatoes take 1 heaping tsp/lb for 15–20 min.
- Soup about 0.9% salt.

## Meat, temperatures, browning, frying (M)
1. **USDA FSIS, Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart.** FSIS's printed chart, read via https://www.geary.k-state.edu/ (2012 PDF) and https://www.bvfb.org/ (2015 web version, printed 2018). Every safe minimum. The live fsis.usda.gov page returned 403.
3. **ThermoWorks, "Chicken internal temps".** https://blog.thermoworks.com/chicken-internal-temps-everything-you-need-to-know/. Time-at-temperature pasteurization table; dark-meat targets.
4. **ThermoWorks, "Carryover cooking".** https://blog.thermoworks.com/carryover-cooking-what-happens-after-you-cook/. Measured carryover after 300°F vs 425°F ovens.
5. **ThermoWorks, "Steak temps".** https://blog.thermoworks.com/steak-temps-getting-it-right/. Doneness chart, pull temps, reverse-sear offsets.
6. **ThermoWorks, "Give your meat a rest".** https://blog.thermoworks.com/give-your-meat-a-rest/. Resting; foil steams the crust; McGee's figure of up to 20°F carryover.
7. **ThermoWorks, chef-recommended pork temps.** https://blog.thermoworks.com/put-chef-recommended-pork-temps-test/. Pull temps by pork cut.
8. **Serious Eats (Daniel Gritzer), controlled resting test, via AOL syndication.** https://www.aol.com/major-rule-cooking-meat-turns-155336379.html. With core temperature controlled, rested meat was no juicier (16 of 30 picks). Resting is temperature management.
9. **ATK, chicken thigh temperature.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/3115. Thighs good at 175°F, meltingly tender at 190–195°F, stringy above 210°F.
10. **ATK, baking soda to tenderize meat.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/6707. 1 tsp per 12 oz sliced meat, ¼ tsp per 12 oz ground, 15 min.
11. **ATK, salt vs brine for chicken.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/610. Brine and dry-brine tables with times.
12. **ATK, crispy-skin whole chicken.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/7572. Slits and holes, baking powder, ≥12 hr uncovered, 450 → 500°F.
13. **ATK, starting in a cold skillet.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/5513. Cold-start chicken thighs.
14. **ATK, best fried chicken.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/8540. Hold oil at 305–315°F; pull temps; cornstarch in the coating.
15. **ATK, "Deep Frying 101".** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/6520. Oil temperature ranges; cool oil gives soggy food.
16. **ATK, light batters.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/7752. Vodka, seltzer and cornstarch limit gluten.
17. **ATK, salmon temperature.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/9999. 125°F farmed, 120°F wild.
18. **ATK, burger dimple.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/5948. A ¼-inch dimple stops doming.
19. **ATK, slicing steak against the grain.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/9580. Bite force is more than 4× with the grain.
20. **Guy Crosby, "A Common Misconception About Frying Food".** https://www.cookingscienceguy.com/. Oil uptake tracks moisture loss and happens on removal; drain on a rack.
21. **Modernist Cuisine, "Understanding Heat".** https://modernistcuisine.com/mc/understanding-heat/. Cook time scales with thickness².
22. **ScienceOfCooking, citing McGee.** https://www.scienceofcooking.com/meat/slow_cooking1.htm. Myosin, collagen and juice-loss temperatures; collagen → gelatin needs time.
23. **Just One Cookbook, tempura (snippet).** https://www.justonecookbook.com/tempura-recipe/. Tempura oil 340–350°F.

Also used, snippets only and unverified: Kenji's wing and reverse-sear methods, smoke-point charts, the Maillard temperature range, smash burgers, and resting breaded food before frying.

## Sauces, starches, vegetables, aromatics (C)
1. **ATK, "How to Fix a Broken Sauce".** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/cooksillustrated/articles/6748. Rescue with ¼ cup water and a vigorous simmer; flour won't fix it.
2. **ATK, finishing pan sauces with butter off heat.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/6685. Butter sauces break above about 160°F; butter is about 16–18% water.
3. **ATK, why cold butter keeps sauces from breaking.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/cooksillustrated/how_tos/8472. Cold butter, 1 tbsp at a time, is the only method that held.
4. **ATK, "Pan Sauces 101".** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/5551. Amounts for fat, shallot, 1 cup liquid → ¾ cup, 2–3 tbsp butter; low-sodium broth.
5. **ATK, Foolproof Vinaigrette.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/4887. 3:1 oil:vinegar with Dijon and mayo as stabilizers.
6. **ATK, Make-Ahead Mayonnaise and How to Fix Broken Mayo.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/12375 and https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/13363. About ¾ cup oil per yolk; the 4-tsp-water rescue.
7. **ATK, sodium citrate explainer and Creamy Mac.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/7649 and https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/5206. Why aged cheese turns grainy; starch and young-cheese fixes.
8. **Modernist Cuisine, "Science helps craft the perfect mac and cheese".** https://modernistcuisine.com/. Sodium citrate 4 g per 100 g cheese plus 93 g liquid.
9. **Milk Street, melted cheese with sodium citrate.** https://www.177milkstreet.com/2020/10/best-melted-cheese-sodium-citrate. 10 g per 1¼ cups milk and 4 cups cheese; doesn't work for Parmesan.
10. **ATK, "Cutting Out Curdling in Cultured Dairy".** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/6302. Sour cream and yogurt curdle near 185°F; crème fraîche doesn't.
11. **ATK, "How to Make Cacio e Pepe".** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/8536. Pecorino works at 145–180°F; use less pasta water, toss off heat.
12. **ATK, pasta tips, salty pasta water, cold-start pasta and the flour-butter hack.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/3372, /articles/5840, /articles/7181, /articles/5544. Gives 1 tbsp table salt per 4 qt; cold start at 1 qt/lb; butter tossed with flour.
13. **Milk Street, perfect pasta and pasta water.** https://www.177milkstreet.com/2022/04/how-to-make-the-perfect-pasta and https://www.177milkstreet.com/stories/01-2019-how-to-use-pasta-water. Pull 1–2 min early; reserve 1 cup; about 2 qt water per lb; no oil.
14. **The Splendid Table, interview with Harold McGee.** https://www.splendidtable.org/story/2011/01/15. Cook pasta in 1.5–2 qt cold water per lb, in a skillet.
15. **ATK, rice ratio, rice science and Everyday White Rice.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/1692, /how-tos/9395, /recipes/11284. Rice absorbs 1:1; evaporation is fixed; 2 cups rice to 3 cups water; rest 10 min.
16. **Milk Street, "How to Cook Rice".** https://www.177milkstreet.com/stories/how-to-cook-rice. Ratios and times by rice type; rinse within 10–15 min of cooking.
17. **ATK thickener pages.** Getting to Know Thickeners (/how_tos/9126), Cornstarch (/articles/7359), Arrowroot (/how_tos/6156), Cornstarch Substitutes (/articles/5498), Three Ways to Thicken (/how_tos/11922), and Milk Temperature for Béchamel (/how_tos/8689). Relative thickening strength, how each starch behaves, roux and béchamel amounts.
18. **King Arthur, pie thickener guide.** https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/learn/guides/pie-thickener. Thickener per cup of fruit; clarity; freezer stability.
19. **ATK, salting watery vegetables.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/4102. ½–1 tsp/lb for 30 min–4 hr; pat dry.
20. **ATK, "How to Roast Any Vegetable".** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/8018. Temperature, oil and timing by vegetable; spacing.
21. **ATK, "Stir-Fry Without a Wok".** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/7725. Batching and the order things go in.
22. **ATK, best-ever blanched vegetables.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/cooksillustrated/how_tos/13338. ¼ cup salt per 2 qt; 2–3 min, then ice water.
23. **ATK, thickening slow-cooker sauces.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/7175. Reduce uncovered, mash some of the solids, or use tapioca.
24. **ATK, "How to Bloom Spices".** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/7628. 30 s–2 min for ground spices; pastes until they darken.
25. **Milk Street, "Bloom Your Spices".** https://www.177milkstreet.com/stories/01-2024-for-punched-up-flavor-bloom-your-spices. Use warm, not hot, fat; about 30 s.
26. **ATK, why you should wait to add garlic.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/7557. Add once the onions are nearly soft; 30–60 s.
27. **ATK, Quick Caramelized Onions.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/7584. True caramelizing takes 1¼ hr; the water-start method takes 25–30 min.
28. **Milk Street, "Brown Tomato Paste".** https://www.177milkstreet.com/2022/03/brown-tomato-paste-umami-flavor. 5–7 min until it darkens and sticks.
29. **ATK, boil vs simmer.** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how-tos/10660. Simmer is 180–190°F; boiling clouds stock.
30. **ATK, "All About Acid".** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/1212. Finish with acid; limit acidic food in cast iron to 30 min.
