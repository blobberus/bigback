# Adjustments: how much to change

Tags like `[S2]` or `[B14]` point to `sources.md`: S is seasoning, M is meat and
heat, C is sauces and vegetables, B is baking. *(heuristic)* marks a judgment call where
no source gave a rule. *(unverified)* marks a number seen only in snippets or on
low-authority pages. Treat both as starting points.

## 1. Size of a seasoning change
- In sensory studies, people first notice a salt change at about 12% in mashed potato,
  14% in mayonnaise, 28% in tomato sauce and 8–12% in cottage cheese *(sensory-study
  snippets, unverified)*. Changes under about 10% are wasted edits.
- How much to cut or add *(heuristic, from those thresholds)*:

| Note | Change |
|---|---|
| "a bit salty" | −10–15% |
| "too salty" | −20–33% |
| "way too salty / inedible" | −50%, or remove the redundant salt source |
| "bland" | +25%, or bring salt toward 1% of the food's weight, or add finishing salt |

- Change one thing at a time and taste after each addition [S15]. Season lightly early
  in anything that reduces, and adjust at the end [S15].
- Per-serving scale: 1% of a 300 g serving is about 3 g of salt, which is about 1 tsp
  Diamond Crystal or ½ tsp table salt (calc).

## 2. Salt
**Brands by weight per teaspoon:**

| Salt | g/tsp | Swap for 1 tsp Diamond Crystal |
|---|---|---|
| Diamond Crystal kosher (DC) | 2.8 [S2] | — |
| Morton kosher | 4.8 [S2] | 0.6 tsp |
| Table salt, fine sea salt | ~6 [S3] | 0.5 tsp |
| Flaky (Maldon) | ~2.5–3 *(unverified)* | finishing only |

- More conversion factors: Morton → table ×0.8. Morton's own chart says 1 tsp table = 1¼
  tsp Morton [S3]. ATK's published factor (DC ×0.75) understates the difference, so go by
  weight [S1][S4]. Ten grams of any salt gives the same salinity [S4].
- A "too salty" note plus "used Morton/table salt" on a DC recipe is a units problem, not
  a recipe problem. Fix the ingredient note.

**Norms:**
- Meat, vegetables and grains: about 1% salt by weight. Blanching or pasta water: up to
  2% (Nosrat) [S14].
- Soups: about 0.9% of the finished weight, range 0.5–1.2% *(unverified)*.
- Burgers: 0.7–1% *(unverified)*. Salt only the outside of the patties, right before
  cooking. Salt mixed in makes them dense and springy (Kenji, via a secondary source).
- Pasta water: 1% = 1 tbsp DC, 2 tsp Morton or 1½ tsp table salt per liter. The usable
  range is 0.5–2%. "Salty as the sea" (3.5%) is far too much [S11].
  - ATK's 1 tbsp table salt per 4 qt (≈0.5%) is the low end [C12].
  - Saltier water carries salt into any sauce you loosen with it [C12]. If a pasta dish
    is salty, cut the water salt first.

**Dry brine (DC amounts, ATK) [S8][M11]:**

| Cut | Salt | Rest |
|---|---|---|
| Steaks and chops | ¾ tsp per 8 oz | ≥1 hr, uncovered on a rack |
| Chicken pieces | ¾ tsp per lb | 6–24 hr |
| Whole chicken, roasts | 1 tsp per lb | 6–24 hr |
| Whole turkey | 1 tsp per lb | 24–48 hr |

- Timing: salt either right before cooking or at least 40 min ahead. At 10–30 minutes the
  brine is still sitting on the surface, which gives a wet, stringy crust [S10].
- Fish: salt no more than about 15 min ahead [S12].

**Wet brine (table salt; halve the time for small pieces) [S6][S7]:**

| Strength | Use | Time |
|---|---|---|
| ~7% (¼ cup per qt) | Chicken pieces or whole bird | 30–60 min (whole bird ~1 hr) |
| ~3.7% (3 tbsp per 1½ qt) | Boneless breasts, pork chops | 30–60 min |
| ~3.7% (¼ cup per 2 qt) | Pork roast | 1½–2 hr |
| ~3.7% (1 cup per 2 gal) | Turkey | 6–12 hr |

  Wet brining hurts skin browning. Over-time brining makes meat too salty. Never brine
  "enhanced" (injected) or pre-salted kosher meat [S6].
- Shrimp: 1 tsp kosher salt + ¼ tsp baking soda per lb, 15–60 min (Serious Eats, via a
  secondary source).

**Hidden salt (count it when adjusting):**
- 1 tbsp soy sauce ≈ 0.4 tsp table salt. 1 tbsp fish sauce ≈ 0.5–0.65 tsp table salt
  (calc from sodium content).
- Parmesan, bacon, olives, capers, regular stock and canned beans are all salty. Reduced
  stock concentrates its salt, so season after reducing.
- Salted butter for unsalted: cut the added salt by ¼ tsp per stick [B3].

## 3. Acid, sugar, fat, heat, umami
- **Flat or heavy dish:** add acid "until it pops" (Nosrat) [S13]. Start with about 1 tsp
  vinegar for a stew pot [S16], or ½ tsp citrus per cup of sauce or soup *(heuristic)*.
  Use cooking acid early (wine to braise or deglaze) and finishing acid at the end [S12].
- **Too sour:** add sugar ¼ tsp per 3 cups, one addition at a time, or fat 1 tbsp at a
  time. In tomato sauce, sugar beat baking soda; even ⅛ tsp soda per 3 cups left it
  "flat, one-note" [S18].
- **Too sweet:** add acid, herbs or a pinch of cayenne. Bitter notes work in desserts
  (espresso powder) [S15]. Avoid "more salt" here: salt suppresses bitterness, which
  makes sweetness stand out more [S16][S19].
- **Too spicy:**
  - In the recipe: cut the chile 33–50%, or remove the ribs and seeds. Cooking chiles
    for 5 minutes noticeably reduces their burn [S21].
  - At the table: dairy, sugar, lime, or more base. Water doesn't help, because
    capsaicin isn't water-soluble [S22].
- **More heat:** raise dried chile about 50% *(heuristic)*, or bloom it in the fat;
  oil extracts more than 2× the capsaicin water does [S20]. Fresh chile added late
  stays hotter [S21]. Cayenne and flakes give the most measurable control. About ¼ tsp
  flakes ≈ 1 jalapeño *(unverified)*.
- **Umami:**
  - Fish sauce: a splash early and a splash at the end. The funk cooks off and the
    glutamate stays [S29].
  - Anchovy: 2 fillets per 3–4 lb of stew, sautéed with the onions.
  - Tomato paste: cook 1–2 min until it darkens [S27].
  - MSG: ¼–½ tsp per 4 servings (0.1–0.8% of the food's weight). It has 12% sodium vs
    39% for salt, so it can replace 25–33% of the salt [S28].

## 4. Herbs and spices
- Dried herbs: ⅓ of the fresh amount, so 1 tsp dried = 1 tbsp fresh [S23]. ATK has also
  suggested ½; the sources disagree.
  - Dried oregano, sage, thyme, rosemary, marjoram and bay work in dishes that simmer
    >20 min.
  - Basil, parsley, chives, dill, tarragon, mint and cilantro need to be fresh,
    especially as a finish [S23][S24].
- Garlic: 1 medium clove = ⅛ tsp garlic powder, or about ¼ tsp granulated [S25].
- Whole spices yield more volume ground (1 tbsp whole →) [S26]:
  - About the same: coriander and fennel 2¾ tsp.
  - Black pepper 3½ tsp, cumin 4½ tsp, mustard 5½ tsp.
  - So about 1:1 when swapping, and up to 1.5× for cumin and mustard.
- Scaled-up batches: scale salt, spices and cayenne to about 75% at first, then taste
  *(unverified heuristic)*.

## 5. Doneness temperatures
**USDA FSIS safe minimums (measure before removing from heat) [M1]:**

| Food | Minimum |
|---|---|
| Beef, pork, veal, lamb (steaks, chops, roasts) | 145°F + 3 min rest |
| Ground meat | 160°F |
| All poultry, incl. ground and stuffing | 165°F |
| Eggs and egg dishes | 160°F |
| Fish and shellfish | 145°F |
| Leftovers, casseroles | 165°F |

**Preferred pull temperatures (they carry over to the final temp):**

| Food | Pull at | Final / note |
|---|---|---|
| Steak, med-rare | 125–130°F | 130–135°F. Rare 120–130, medium 135–145, medium-well 145–155 [M5] |
| Pork loin, chops, tenderloin | 135–140°F | ~145°F [M7][M8] |
| Chicken breast | 155–160°F | Safe by time-at-temperature hold (below) [M3] |
| Thighs, legs | 175–195°F | 175°F is good, 190–195°F is meltingly tender, >210°F turns stringy [M9] |
| Salmon | 120°F wild, 125°F farmed | 145°F (USDA) reads as dry [M17] |
| Braises, pulled pork, brisket | 195–205°F | Probe-tender. 145°F is safe but tough [M22] |
| Fried chicken | white 145°F, dark 160°F | Coasts to ~165°F and 180–190°F over a 20-min rest [M14] |

- Chicken hold times. These are equally safe as 165°F instant [M3]:

| Temp | Hold |
|---|---|
| 145°F | 9.8 min |
| 150°F | 3 min |
| 155°F | 50 s |
| 157°F | 31 s |
| 160°F | 15 s |

  Below 165°F, the recipe text must say "and holds there while it rests".
- Carryover: thin cuts rise 3–5°F, roasts 10–15°F, and up to 20°F after very hot
  cooking [M5][M6].
  - Hotter cooking means bigger carryover. Measured: a pork loin pulled at 140°F rose
    6.5°F after a 300°F oven vs 15.5°F after a 425°F oven. Salmon rose 7°F vs 19°F [M4].
  - When the oven temperature goes up, pull earlier.

## 6. Time, heat and oven
- **Thickness, not weight, sets cook time.** Time scales with roughly thickness²: 2×
  thicker takes about 4× as long [M21]. For the same shape, time grows with weight^⅔, so
  2× the weight takes about 1.6× as long (calc).
- **Burnt outside, raw inside:** lower the heat, or sear and then finish in a 325–350°F
  oven. Or cut thinner or more evenly. Don't just extend the time [M13][M21].
- **Ovens** cycle ±25°F and can be off by more. Most "my oven runs hot" fixes belong in
  the cook's oven, not the recipe [B23].
  - Preheat ≥20 min at ≤350°F and ≥30 min above that; the beep comes early [B18][B23].
  - Convection: −25°F and check early. Good for roasting and browning, bad for cakes and
    custards [B30].
  - Glass pans and truly dark pans: −25°F [B16][B17].
- **Hotter oven for precooked or heat-through dishes** (casseroles, gratins): about 10–15%
  less time per +25°F *(heuristic)*, and keep foil on longer so the top doesn't scorch.
  Raw proteins: retime by thermometer, not formula.
- **Altitude, >3,000 ft (KA) [B19]:**

| Adjustment | Amount |
|---|---|
| Oven | +15–25°F |
| Bake time | −5–8 min per 30 min |
| Sugar | −1 tbsp per cup |
| Liquid | +1–2 tbsp, then +1½ tsp per extra 1,000 ft |
| Flour | +1 tbsp at 3,500 ft, then +1 tbsp per extra 1,500 ft |
| Yeast | −25% |
| Leavener, per 1 tsp | ⅞ tsp (3–5k ft), ½ (5–6.5k), ¼ (6.5–8k) |

## 7. Baking ratios and leavening
- **Weights per cup (KA) [B1]:**

| Ingredient | g/cup |
|---|---|
| AP or bread flour | 120 |
| Whole wheat flour | 113 |
| Cake flour | 120 |
| Granulated sugar | 198 |
| Packed brown sugar | 213 |
| Confectioners' sugar | 113 |
| Butter | 226 (stick = 113) |
| Cocoa | 84 |
| Oil | 198 |
| Milk, water, buttermilk, yogurt | 227 |
| Honey | 336 |

  - A large egg is 50 g (white 30–35, yolk 14). Baking powder 4 g/tsp, baking soda
    6 g/tsp.
  - Scooped flour runs +20–33% (up to 160 g per cup). That's the #1 cause of dry,
    dense or tough baking. Rewrite flour as grams plus spoon-and-level cups [B2][B4].
- **Leavening per cup of flour:** baking powder 1–1¼ tsp; baking soda ¼ tsp. Soda needs
  acid: ½ tsp per 1 cup buttermilk, yogurt or sour cream [B34][B37].
  - Soda is about 3–4× stronger than baking powder.
  - Too much baking powder: bitter, rises then collapses. Too much soda: soapy, coarse,
    over-browned, flat [B36].
  - Freshness test: soda fizzes in vinegar; powder bubbles in hot water [B12].
- **Ruhlman ratios, by weight [B39–B43]:**

| Item | Ratio |
|---|---|
| Bread | 5 flour : 3 water (+ salt, yeast) |
| Pasta | 3 flour : 2 egg |
| Pie dough | 3 flour : 2 fat : 1 water |
| Biscuit | 3 flour : 1 fat : 2 liquid |
| Cookie | 1 sugar : 2 fat : 3 flour |
| Pound cake | 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 |
| Muffin | 2 flour : 2 liquid : 1 egg : 1 butter |
| Pancake | 2 liquid : 1 egg : ½ butter : 2 flour |
| Crêpe | 2 egg : 2 milk : 1 flour |
| Choux | 2 water : 1 butter : 1 flour : 2 egg |
| Custard | 2 dairy : 1 egg |
| Vinaigrette | 3 oil : 1 acid |

  A recipe far off its ratio explains a texture complaint.
- **Doneness temps for baked goods [B21][B31]:**

| Item | Done at |
|---|---|
| Lean bread | 200–210°F |
| Enriched bread | 185–195°F |
| Cakes | 200–209°F |
| Quick breads, muffins | 200–205°F |
| Brownies | ~200°F |
| Cheesecake | 150°F (NY-style 165°F) |
| Baked custard | 170–175°F |

  Bread can hit its temperature before the crust is done; also go by color [B20].

## 8. Scaling
The UI's serving scaler multiplies quantities linearly. When notes come after scaling
("doubled it and…"), check for these non-linear effects:
- **Evaporation is fixed by pot area, not volume.** Doubled rice needs less than 2× the
  water: 1½ cups rice + 2¼ cups water doubles to 3 cups + 3¾ cups, not 4½ [B25]. Doubled
  braises and beans take longer to reduce [B24].
- **Browning:** more meat in the same pan steams. Sear in batches [B25].
- **Baking:** individually portioned items (cookies, muffins, biscuits) scale fine.
  Bake cakes and loaves in more pans of the same size, not one bigger pan [B24].
  For a smaller batch, start checking at half the original time [B14].
- **Seasoning:** taste instead of multiplying (§4). Leavening doubles fine in cookies (KA, snippet).
- **Eggs for partial batches:** whisk, then weigh about 50 g per egg [B14].
- **Pan math:** factor = new area ÷ old area, at the same batter depth [B33].

| Pan | Area (sq in) | Cups |
|---|---|---|
| 8" round | 50 | 6 |
| 9" round | 64 | 8 |
| 8" square | 64 | 8 |
| 9" square | 81 | 10 |
| 11×7 | 77 | 10 |
| 9×13 | 117 | 14 |
| 9×5 loaf | 45 | 8 |
| 8½×4½ loaf | 38 | 6 |

  - A 9" round and an 8" square swap freely. Half a 9×13 is about 58 sq in.
  - Fill pans ½–⅔ full [B15][B35].
  - Deeper or smaller pan: +10–15 min (and some bakers drop the oven 25°F).
  - Shallower or larger pan: check 10–15 min early [B15][B33].
  - Don't swap pans for Bundt, angel food or cheesecake.

## 9. Thickening, sauces, starches
**Thickener strength.** Equivalents for 1 tbsp cornstarch [C17]:

| Thickener | Amount |
|---|---|
| AP flour | 2 tbsp (flour is ~75% starch) |
| Arrowroot | 1½ tbsp |
| Potato starch | 1–1½ tbsp |
| Tapioca starch | 1 tbsp |

**How each one behaves** [C17]:
- **Cornstarch:** use a cold slurry. It needs about 1 min at a boil to thicken fully and
  thins again with long cooking. The finish is glossy.
  - Start with about 1 tsp per cup of liquid and add more gradually. The "1 tbsp per
    cup" rule gives a thick result and is *(unverified)*.
  - It weeps after freezing.
- **Arrowroot:** clear, and thickens below a boil. It turns slimy with dairy.
- **Potato starch:** add it last and stop cooking once it thickens.
- **Instant tapioca:** holds through long, slow cooking (slow cookers). Flour and
  cornstarch don't.

**Roux** (equal parts fat and flour) [C17]:
- Béchamel: about 1 tbsp each per cup of milk; +1 tsp flour per cup for baked dishes.
  Whisk in cold milk (fewer lumps), then boil 2 min.
- Gravy: 2 tbsp fat + 2 tbsp flour per cup of stock. Darker roux thickens less.
- Thin / medium / thick: 1 / 2 / 3 tbsp each per cup *(unverified convention)*.

**Last-minute fixes:**
- Beurre manié: equal volumes of flour and soft butter, 1 tbsp at a time, simmered
  2–3 min [C12].
- Flour-butter hack: 3 tbsp cubed butter tossed with 1 tsp flour, whisked into the
  simmering sauce over 1½–2 min [C12].
- Reduction thickens without starch, but it also concentrates salt.
- Sauce too thick: thin with stock or pasta water a spoonful at a time [C4][C13].

**Pan sauce** [C2][C3][C4]:
- Pour off all but ~1 tbsp fat. Add aromatics. Deglaze with ~1 cup low-sodium liquid.
- Reduce to ~¾ cup (about 3 min).
- Off the heat, whisk in 2–3 tbsp cold butter, 1 tbsp at a time.
- Butter sauces break above about 160°F, so don't boil after the butter goes in.

**Emulsions:**
- Vinaigrette: 3 oil : 1 acid, plus ½ tsp Dijon and ½ tsp mayo per tbsp vinegar as
  stabilizers [C5].
- Mayonnaise: about ¾ cup oil per yolk [C6].

**Cheese sauce:**
- Sodium citrate: 2.8% of the cheese + liquid weight [C8], or 10 g per 1¼ cups milk +
  4 cups cheese [C9]. It doesn't work for Parmesan.
- Cultured dairy: sour cream and yogurt curdle near 185°F [C10]. Add them off the heat.

**Pasta:**
- Reserve ≥1 cup of pasta water. Pull the pasta 1–2 min before the package time and
  finish it in the sauce [C13].
- Cold start: 1 qt water per lb is starchier and up to 45% faster [C12].
- Cacio e pepe: the cheese must stay between 145 and 180°F [C11].

**Rice** (water : rice, rinsed) [C16]:

| Rice | Water : rice | Cook | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long-grain white | 1.5 : 1 | ~15 min | — |
| Jasmine | 1.33 : 1 | 15–18 min | — |
| Basmati | 1.5 : 1 | 15–18 min | — |
| Short-grain | 1.17 : 1 | ~12 min | 10–15 min |
| Brown | 1.75 : 1 | ~25 min | 10 min |

- ATK long-grain: 2 cups rice : 3 cups water, 20 min on low, then rest 10 min without
  lifting the lid [C15].
- Rice absorbs about 1:1. The rest of the water replaces evaporation, so scaled batches
  need proportionally less [C15].

**Vegetables:**
- Salt watery vegetables at ½–1 tsp per lb for 30 min–4 hr, then pat dry [C19].
- Roast with 1–2 tbsp oil per lb, in a single spaced layer [C20]:
  - 425°F: squash, carrots.
  - 450°F: green beans, mushrooms.
  - 475–500°F: broccoli, cauliflower, sprouts, asparagus.
- Blanch in ¼ cup salt per 2 qt water for 2–3 min, then shock in ice water [C22].
